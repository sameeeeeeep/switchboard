it's an app store.

context. through one consent gate you own.

- no account to create
- nothing leaves your Mac without a row you approve
- your context carries from one app to the next instead of dying in each silo
- 94 apps in the store, all free, all running on your own AI

a PM who couldn't code didn't build an app. he built the thing the apps run on.

more soon — i'll show you the first few wrapps this week.

market sizing, competitor teardown, positioning, naming, a go-to-market plan.

shared. and the context you build in one carries straight into the next app.

minus-one to zero, in the time it takes to make coffee.

# Post 2 — ADHD as a superpower

**Screenshare cue:** paste a messy brain-dump into Tasks → watch it become deduped, ordered
kanban cards with blockers marked → answer an a/b/c decision card at the notch with one
keystroke. Show the "1a 2c" reply pattern if you can.

### LinkedIn

i have ADHD.

for most of my life that meant fifteen tabs open, forty ideas a day, and finishing roughly
none of them.

so instead of fighting it, i built a Claude Code setup that treats it like the superpower it
actually is — and files down the part that isn't.

three pieces do most of the work:

→ **ADHD PM** — i dump everything in my head, unsorted, and it comes back deduped, prioritized,
and decision-ready. no wall of questions. it hands me a/b/c options with one ⭐ recommended
pick, and i just answer "1a 2c." the ideas were never the problem. the sorting was.

→ **Tasks** — a real kanban over my own files. paste a scattered brain-dump and it becomes
ordered cards with the blockers marked between them, parked until i'm ready to pull them.

→ **Guru** — a live guide that walks me through the thing i keep bouncing off, step by step,
so i actually finish it.

the whole point: it meets the way my brain works instead of asking my brain to change.

and the best part — same as everything i build — it's free to use on your own Claude with
Switchboard. your board, your files, your machine.

if your brain works like mine, this one's for you.

# Post 3 — Flow (the Wispr Flow story)

**Screenshare cue:** hold ⌃⌥, talk, watch it type into whatever app you're in — a Slack box, a
doc, a terminal. Then ⌥V to re-paste the last dictation. Show it's on-device.

### LinkedIn

i loved Wispr Flow.

but there was no way my indian ass was paying a subscription for what is, fundamentally,
talking and having it typed.

so i built my own. hold a key, talk, and it types wherever your cursor is — any app on the
Mac, transcribed on-device. there's even a voice clipboard (⌥V) to re-paste your last one.

but here's the ADHD tax: i can't build one thing and stop.

so it's not a standalone dictation app you'd have to — yes — make another account for. it's
one of the apps inside Switchboard, running on your own model, sharing context with everything
else i've built. the dictation you just spoke can feed straight into the next app in the box.

turns out the thing i was too cheap to pay for became the reason i shipped a whole platform.

worth it.

# Post 5 — Guru / guided touch

**Screenshare cue:** hit a wall where Claude says "I can't do that for you" → trigger Guru →
it points a cursor at the real screen, step by step, and you do the click it couldn't. Show
it adapting when the screen isn't what it expected.

### LinkedIn

i use Claude for everything. which means i've hit the wall everyone hits:

"here are the steps — you'll need to do this part yourself."

grant a permission. click a native app it can't reach. flip a setting in a UI it can't touch.
the moment the work leaves the chat, you're on your own with a list of instructions.

that gap drove me up the wall. so i built the bridge.

it's called Guru — a live guide that takes Claude's instructions and walks you through them on
the *actual app*, step by step. it points a cursor at the real thing on your screen, waits for
you to do it, and adapts when your screen isn't what it expected. a static how-to can't do that.
a manual can't do that.

so Claude stops handing you homework. the instruction becomes a guided touch — the two of you
finishing the task together, on the real thing, instead of it stopping at the edge of what it
can reach.

it's built into Switchboard, free, on your own Claude. like the rest.
