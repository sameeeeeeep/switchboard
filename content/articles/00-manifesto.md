# The Last Prompt

*a manifesto, told as the story of Sam Kurzwala — who built an AI to see his own future, and learned what it takes to make science fiction real.*

---

Sam Kurzwala wanted to know what happened next.

Not the stock market. Not the weather. His own life — the next year of it, the fork he couldn't see yet, the version of himself waiting on the other side of a decision he hadn't made. So he did what people like him do now: he built an AI to tell him.

He called it a personal future-prediction engine, which is a grand name for what began as a nightly cron job. Every night it read everything Sam had touched that day — his notes, his messages, his half-finished projects, the shape of his attention — and every morning it told him a little more about where he was heading.

For a while it was a party trick. Then it started being right.

But it kept hitting the same wall, and the wall was Sam.

The AI could only ever see as far as Sam reached for it. It woke, it thought, it went back to sleep — waiting for him to ask the next question. Every insight needed a human to summon it. His future-prediction engine could not act on the future it predicted. It was a genius on a leash, and the leash was the prompt.

That's when Sam understood the thing this whole field keeps dancing around:

**Science fiction doesn't run on prompts.**

The AI we were promised — the one that discovers, invents, protects, predicts — doesn't wait to be asked. It runs on its own. It improves itself. It operates. The dream was never a smarter autocomplete; it was an intelligence that *does things while you sleep* and is better tomorrow than it was today. Self-improving. Autonomous. Awake to its own goals.

And the first step toward that — the specific, unglamorous thing standing between us and all of it — is this:

**A promptless AI.** One that no longer needs you to type the next thing.

Sam pulled on that thread, and it unravelled the whole industry.

Because the reason AI can't run on its own isn't intelligence. The models are already capable of far more than we let them do. The reason is that every *piece* of AI is trapped in its own box. The model is one subscription. The context lives in another app. The interface is a chat window that forgets. The harness that lets it hold tools is welded to one vendor. Your data, your agents, your identity — each behind its own wall — and *you* are the wire running between them. You are the router. Prompt by prompt, tab by tab, you are the integration layer holding the future together with your own two hands.

Someone already noticed this for one piece of it. MCP made **connectors** routable — a universal way for any model to reach any tool. It was a genuine revelation. It was also one plug on a switchboard with a hundred sockets.

Because connectors are only the start. To get a promptless, self-improving, autonomous AI, you have to make *all* of it routable — not just the tools, but the **harnesses, the interfaces, the subscriptions, the models, the context, the data, the users, the agents.** Every component of intelligence: addressable, swappable, composable. A substrate an AI can stand on and *run* — instead of a human hand-carrying it between silos.

That substrate is what Sam started building. It's what we're building.

We call the lab **The Last Prompt**, and we mean it two ways.

It's the last prompt because that's the goal: the final thing you'll ever have to type — the prompt that makes prompting unnecessary, that hands the future-prediction engine its own legs.

And it's the last prompt because someone has to write it first. That's the work. That's the fiction we're trying to make true.

Path one already exists. It's called **Switchboard**, and it does for the rest of the AI stack what MCP did for connectors: it makes your models, your subscription, your context, your interfaces, your users and your agents *routable*. One board. Yours.

Sam Kurzwala was never a special person. Sam Kurzwala is what happens when an ordinary one stops waiting for the future to be handed to him and starts wiring it together himself.

We're an AI lab building science fiction.
