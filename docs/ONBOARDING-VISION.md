# Onboarding Vision — the birth of your operator (not a tutorial)

Written 2026-08-28 after the founder pushed past "a nicer takeover" to "think deeper."

## The reframe
Every prior concept — floating cards, the panel, even the cinematic screen-owning "Wire-In" — is
underneath a TUTORIAL: it teaches features (connect, senses, hotkeys) in a prettier wrapper. That is the
shallowness. The depth is refusing to make onboarding a tutorial at all.

Switchboard's thesis is **the last prompt** — the user crossing the line from "I use an AI I prompt" to
"I have an operator that acts for me." Onboarding's real job is to **manufacture the first experience of
DELEGATION** — the single moment that flips the paradigm. Everything else (permissions, hotkeys) is
plumbing that must be invisible or in service of that one moment.

## The shape
1. **First contact — the operator is BORN, it doesn't "welcome."** The screen-owning overlay = your
   operator stepping forward to meet you. It TALKS (a presence you talk to, not a wall of buttons):
   "I'm yours. I run on your Claude, right here — nothing leaves this machine. What are you working on?"
2. **It learns YOU — becomes yours in the first 20s.** A real exchange (spoken/typed), not a form. It
   uses your words IMMEDIATELY to personalize — your context, your first real task. Not a demo. Yours.
3. **First delegation — the "holy shit."** From what you just told it, it does a REAL thing: opens the
   right app on YOUR task, acts across YOUR apps, live. Not sample.m4a. The overlay peels back to reveal
   real work happening. This is the moment it stops being a chatbot and becomes an operator.
4. **It CAN'T stop — because your operator doesn't stop.** No "you're all set" card. As the work lands,
   the overlay THINS — the operator recedes into the notch: "Press ⌃⌃ to bring me back." Then it stays,
   working. Your first session IS the onboarding, continuing, scaffolding dissolving as you go.
5. **Scaffolding fades over days, not at a finish line.** It surfaces the next capability the moment it's
   relevant, less and less. No "done" — a relationship that gets quieter as you get fluent.

## Why this is the deep version
The prior concepts owned the screen and looked good, but they were ME TEACHING YOU THE APP. This is your
operator being BORN, LEARNING you, DOING your first real thing, and NEVER LEAVING — literally the product
(an operator, not a tool; the last prompt). The overlay is it taking the stage; the recede is it becoming
ambient; "can't stop" is because an operator doesn't stop.

## What this demands (harder than a tour)
- Real conversational first-contact (voice + text), not scripted steps.
- Capturing identity/intent and PERSONALIZING from it live (seeds the vault/context).
- A real first DELEGATION: the operator resolving the user's stated intent to a real action across real
  apps (the guru/orchestration capability, aimed at a cold-start user) — with an honest fallback when it
  can't fully act yet.
- An overlay that is a living FRAME (owns → breaks out to real surfaces → thins → recedes to notch), not
  a slideshow — and a fading, contextual scaffolding that persists past the first session.
