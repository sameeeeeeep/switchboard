01 — One-liner & thesis

The last prompt

Superintelligent models are throttled not by capability but by the prompts and interfaces we wrap around them. Switchboard is the private, personalised gateway that lets you run your own AI — your Claude subscription, your local models, your connected tools — across any app or tool, with zero added lag and at no cost beyond the subscriptions you already pay for.

The insight that reframes everything

“These are actually skins for Claude Code if you think about it.” If every app is a skin over the user's own model, the app can never be the product — anyone can clone a skin in an afternoon. The durable product is the wire: your Claude + your files + the private, consented connection between them. Switchboard sells the wire; the apps are numbers you can dial.

The metaphor. Today's AI apps are walled telephone networks — each wants to be the one place you live, your data trapped inside, switching costs total. A telephone switchboard connects a call across networks without owning your phone or listening in. Switchboard is the operator's board for AI: it connects without owning. Infrastructure, not a destination — the thing that ends the walled garden rather than being another wall.

What it opens. One private gateway unlocks an open, growing ecosystem of wrapps — apps that bring nothing but the interface and borrow the user's own AI to do the work. The repo already carries 76 wrapps in the catalog. The named surfaces below (Flow, God, Guru, Autopilot, Bank) are the first few, not a closed list.

Switchboard connects without owning. Your Claude runs the call, your files stay home, the board never hears a word.

02 — The problem

Apps resell you what you already own — and there is no consent layer

Double payment
You pay twice for the same intelligence

You already pay for Claude, for GPT, for local compute. Every AI app then charges you again to route a prompt through a model you're already subscribed to. The app operator provisions and marks up inference you could run yourself.

Data leaves
Your data and compute leave your machine

To use an app, your files, context, and prompts get uploaded to someone else's server. The app holds your credentials, sees your content, and becomes the place your work is trapped. Switching apps means abandoning that data.

No gate
There is no consent layer for AI

When an AI agent acts — sends, buys, publishes, deletes — nothing stands between the model and the irreversible action but the app's own good behaviour. There is no user-owned broker that classifies, gates, budgets, and audits every action out-of-band.

Each of these is a wall. The economic wall (you pay the operator's margin), the data wall (your work lives on their disk), and the trust wall (you must trust their code with your credentials and your consequences). Switchboard removes all three by inverting who holds the resources: the user's machine is the backend, and a broker on that machine — not the app, not the model — is the single point of enforcement.

Persona A · The founder-operator

Lives away from the terminal. Wants decisions surfaced as clean A/B/C options, work drafted overnight, and a short approve-queue of the few calls only they can make. Installs the gateway once, then opens any wrapp and clicks Connect. Reads run silently within a scope they approved; writes ask for a click. Their Claude runs the work, their files stay home, and their spend is metered honestly in their own panel.

Persona C · The wrapp developer

JTBD: “Ship an app with real AI, storage, and data behind it — without provisioning a backend, holding anyone's keys, or paying an inference bill.”

Codes against a single stable provider (@relay/sdk). Declares the scope the app needs; the broker gates it. Distribution is a static page. The economic inversion — the visitor's own subscription covers inference — is what makes “I could build this myself” collapse into “why would I.”

05 — The moat / security model

The consent broker is the product

The moat is not the catalog — a skin is clonable in an afternoon. The moat is the broker: a user-owned enforcement point that no app, and no model, can talk its way past. These invariants are non-negotiable. Every feature in this document is built to preserve them.

The seven invariants

  1. The extension is the origin oracle. A request's origin is the browser-verified sender (content-script sender), never data the page supplies. Every per-origin grant, budget, and storage path keys on it. A page cannot claim to be another origin.
  2. The daemon is the only enforcement point — never the model. Allowlists, read/write classification, budgets, and per-action consent are checked out-of-band in the daemon. No prompt in model context can widen scope. The model receives a hard --allowed-tools capability set.
  3. Two consent tiers. Reads are pre-approvable within a scope. Writes / irreversible / money actions raise a per-action consent the user must click every time — non-bypassable. Classification comes from a daemon-side policy table that is default-deny: an unknown tool is treated as dangerous.
  4. The gate lives inside the agentic loop. The daemon runs the Agent SDK's query() in-process and wires its canUseTool callback straight into the gate. It fires out-of-band for every proposed tool (MCP tools and builtins), is async (can block on a human click), and enforces deny. bypassPermissions is never used; tools are never pre-allowed around the gate.
  5. Secrets never cross the boundary. The API key and connector credentials stay with the daemon and the MCP processes. The page receives results only — never keys. (Credential-injected HTTP, when it lands, sets the auth header daemon-side; the page still never sees the token.)
  6. Audit + control. Every act appends to an append-only, per-origin audit log (~/.relay/audit.log), exportable. Per-origin revoke; a global kill switch; delete the pairing token to lock out every paired browser.
  7. Exact-match, fail-closed grants. A model or tool not literally in the grant is denied — full stop (SCOPE_EXCEEDED). Grants narrow (the user may approve a subset); they never implicitly widen. Consent times out closed.

Structural isolation, not runtime checks. State is partitioned by the authoritative origin at the path/handle level — storage derives its folder from the origin (folderFor(origin)), so one origin's data can never resolve into another's. Cross-origin sharing exists only through the explicit, consented context vault.

The honest boundary (repeated across all the code)

Switchboard drafts and decides; a human gates every irreversible action; connectors — never the engine — act. The vision's “makes the moves / runs your life” language runs ahead of the merged code. What is actually load-bearing, and what is the moat, is the gate, the audit log, and the per-origin grant. Keep the copy inside that boundary.

06 — Architecture

A thin broker, a versioned bus, pluggable backends

wrapp (untrusted page) │ window.claude.request({method, params}) ← EIP-1193-style bus, feature-detected like window.ethereum ▼ extension (MV3) — the origin oracle; stamps the browser-verified origin + tabId │ loopback WebSocket + pairing token (the page never touches the daemon directly) ▼ daemon (sidekick) ── verify origin → look up grant → classify (read/write) → gate → audit │ ├─ backends: Claude Code (reference) · local-openai (Ollama/LM Studio) · OpenRouter (hosted, opt-in) ├─ MCP registry: ~/.claude.json servers + claude.ai connectors (inherited by the SDK, no bridge) ├─ primitives: storage · context · session · put_blob └─ Run layer: routines registry (the clock) → routineDraft / routineInvoke / routineDispatch

The provider (window.claude)

A single typed, versioned method bus injected into allowlisted pages — deliberately shaped like window.ethereum so apps feature-detect one recognizable global. It carries far more than Claude: inference, storage, context, sessions, local TTS, identity. The wire protocol is BYOP 1.0.0; developers code against @relay/sdk, never the raw global (a neutral window.switchboard alias is on the roadmap). Injected as a web-accessible MAIN-world script — the reliable wallet pattern.

The broker / daemon

Runs as a macOS LaunchAgent (com.relay.sidekick, RunAtLoad + KeepAlive). Ships as a single ~2.2 MB esbuild bundle carrying the whole daemon + the Agent SDK, alongside a copied Node runtime and Anthropic's own signed CLI, inside a signed + notarized DMG. The trusted core is a small router + consent enforcer; capabilities are meant to be pluggable modules behind a registry so adding one never touches the core.

Routing rule that protects the promise: an omitted or unknown model id resolves to the user's own default, never silently to a hosted backend. Hosted models are reachable only by exact id, so nothing goes off-machine without an opt-in.

MCP registry & connectors

Two connector classes. (1) Local stdio/http MCP servers auto-imported from ~/.claude.json — reachable by both the direct callTool path and the agentic path. (2) claude.ai connectors (Higgsfield, Shopify, …) — inherited automatically by the daemon's SDK from the user's sign-in, no bridge, reachable via the agentic path. A whole connector is granted with a wildcard tool grant (mcp__claude_ai_X__*); each concrete call is still classified (generate_* = write → consent; *_status/display = read → auto).

The Run layer (routines)

The daemon's scheduler — the piece that makes “runs while every tab is closed” real. A RoutineRegistry runs a 15s master heartbeat; each routine fires on its own interval, writes status to ~/.relay/routines.json, and reads ~/.relay/routines-control.json each sweep so a pause takes effect within one heartbeat. Ships dormant ({off:true}) — nothing runs in the background until the user flips the master switch. Two tiers: the daemon tier (built — gated, audited first-party drafts) and the Claude-Code tier (next — a full agent that could edit code / open a draft PR). A routine never sends: the send line holds regardless of tier.

Reference: packages/sidekick/src/routines/{registry.ts,autopilot.ts}; broker seams routineDraft / routineInvoke / routineDispatch in server.ts. Slice proven live: master switch on → autopilot ticked → drafted 3 real moves (~22k real tokens) → switch off → froze.

The switchboard connector (God's Hands reuse)

Rather than re-implement each wrapp headlessly, a wrapp declares its God-callable actions once (exposeToGod → WebMCP registerTool + window.__god), and God hosts the wrapp in a surface it controls and drives its real UI. The switchboard MCP connector re-exposes those curated, DRAFT-class wrapp actions as mcp__…__wrapp__* tools — the reverse-flow seam by which a routine (or God) runs a real wrapp on the user's own Claude, audited as routine@autopilot. The seam is hardened: routineInvoke hard-checks the __wrapp__ prefix, so a routine can only reach the curated reversible surface — never a non-wrapp or an outward tool. The connector (packages/switchboard-mcp) advertises one tool per wrapp action and currently ships 6 wrapp manifests (adpulse, batch, redline, autopilot, ideabrain, reachout); discovery is opt-in — the list itself is the gate.

Storage — per-origin, files-as-records

A per-origin key/value store gated like everything else. Each origin gets a private sandbox (~/.relay/storage/<origin>/) with no prompt; bind points an origin at a real user folder behind a one-time path-consent. Keys are filenames — a key maps 1:1 to <key>.json, so an existing project folder's files appear as records with zero migration, and a bound folder doubles as a real Obsidian vault or project source. (A colon in a key is illegal and silently fails — a real bug class that took out a batch of wrapps' persistence tier.)

08 — The wrapp ecosystem

76 wrapps, four layers, one wire

A wrapp is a web (or native) app that borrows the user's own AI, storage, and data through Switchboard instead of provisioning a backend itself. Because it's a skin over the user's Claude, its value isn't the code — it's the interface, the prompts, the workflow. The wire is what's durable.

The four-layer model

Layer 1
Capabilities

The factored primitives a wrapp strings together — complete, stream, storage, context, tools, TTS.

Layer 2
Workflow

The orchestration — the run(input, sb) pipeline: single input → options + ⭐ recommended.

Layer 3
UI shell

The house template — plumbing, stream contract, timeouts, context sync, the standard connect chip.

Layer 4
Skin

The presentation — a live-switchable renderer + design tokens over one catalog.

What ships in the catalog

The catalog carries 76 listings (examples/apps/wrapps/catalog.json), each with id · name · tagline · category · components · surfaces · requires · tools. Wrapp doctrine: context-first (via Switchboard), a single input, options with a recommended pick, and the brandbrain design system. On-device wrapps that need no AI (convert / resize / QR) carry egressTier: none — they never slow the device.

Flagships
Flow · Cartridge · brandbrain

Native dictation; form→playable HTML5 game on your Claude; the brand studio (full port in progress).

Verbs
ideabrain · autopilot · redline

Should-this-exist; runs-it; audits-a-page. Each a distinct verb, not a mode of another.

On-device
convert · resize · QR

Genuinely no-AI, no-egress utilities — the device-lightness rule made visible.

Distribution & symmetry

  • Subdomain deploy. Each wrapp is its own Pages repo (<wrapp>.thelastprompt.ai), which isolates both grants and reputation — a Safe-Browsing flag is host-level, so per-wrapp subdomains stop one bad wrapp nuking the rest. The extension origin allowlist must include a wrapp's origin (in both manifest lists) before window.claude injects.
  • WebMCP symmetry. One actions declaration renders three ways: the DOM UI, the daemon's headless wrapp__* MCP tools, and WebMCP tools in-browser (Gemini-in-Chrome can already drive them). The consuming half — user-owned agent drives any page's tools under the user's gate — is the open first-mover slot. Aspirational

10 — Business model

Charge for infrastructure, never for AI

The rule that keeps it coherent

Your infra is free. Our cloud is Pro. We charge for infrastructure we run — never for AI access. AI stays bring-your-own forever. If a price ever attaches to the model itself, the pitch is dead.

Privacy-led positioning — the five noes

Solo Switchboard is free forever: BYO Claude, local models, the whole wrapp catalog. The front door is free + 100% private; paying is a small side door. The honest phrasing: with cloud backup on, “nothing leaves your machine” becomes “nothing readable leaves your machine” — a stronger posture than every collaboration product that reads everything.

The three independently-priced things

Three separate lines, never one bundle. Sync and storage are near-zero fixed cost; inference is real variable cost, so it must never ride inside a cheap flat tier. The entitlement carries capabilities, not a plan name. Billing (Stripe / Paddle MoR → mint an entitlement token) is the one piece not built; the HMAC gate that enforces it already refuses correctly.

The creator economy — Spotify, not app-store IAP

Aspirational · direction only Users pay one Switchboard Pro tier. Publishers don't earn per-use — they aren't hosting; it's a true library, run on the user's own compute. Instead, like streams: Switchboard pools Pro revenue, keeps a small ~25% cut, and distributes ~75% to devs by their metered usage share. This is only honest because the broker is the meter — it already sees real per-wrapp token spend, un-fakeably. That same metering shows real build-cost + run-cost receipts on store cards (the visible buy-vs-build math), with a hard honesty law: only meter-measured numbers get the badge; off-platform builds show “unmeasured,” never an estimate dressed as data.

Enterprise is the cash line: governance (audit / scopes / budgets / kill switch) + custom wrapps + implementation as paid services on the same broker.

12 — Success metrics & risks

What proves it, and what could sink it

Success metrics

Activation
Install → first successful action

Fraction of installs that clear the whole ladder (extension + DMG + pair + Claude signed-in + connect) and land one real completion. The install journey is the sharpest known drop-off — the signed-out cliff especially.

Trust
Consent-grant rate & retention

Do users approve the connect scope, keep grants over time, and use the per-action gate without disabling wrapps? Grants revoked ≈ trust lost.

Depth
Metered usage per user & per wrapp

Real broker-measured tokens/tool-calls — the un-fakeable substrate for both product health and the future payout. Background (routine) spend is metered and visible, never hidden.

Ecosystem
Wrapps published & dialed

Catalog growth, plus how many wrapps a user actually connects — the “numbers you can dial” getting dialed. Developer publishes → first earning is a zero AI bill.
